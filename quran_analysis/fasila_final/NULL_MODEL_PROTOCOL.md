# Null-model protocol

All null models evaluate the same observed quantity: for each of the 49 distinct verses in the 30 eligible pairs, whether the verse's **actual** final-phoneme key (definition D1, pausal) occurs among the D1 keys of its k = 3 neighbours within the same surah (`LOC`). The observed own-match rate is 0.796 (39/49 distinct verses; 48/60 when verses are counted once per pair). Each null replaces the actual ending with an alternative drawn from a specified pool and recomputes the match rate; 10,000 Monte-Carlo draws; p = share of draws ≥ observed. The pool defines what is controlled; the final phoneme itself is never matched.

| Model | Pool for the alternative ending | Controls | What remains tested | Result (own 0.796) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **N0** global frequency | all 6,236 verse endings of the Quran | ending frequency | whether the ending fits the local pattern | null 0.356 [0.245, 0.469], p < 10⁻⁴ | sensitivity |
| **N1a** partner-surah frequency | all endings of the *partner* verse's surah | surah-specific rhyme repertoire of the counterfactual source | whether the ending fits *this* surah rather than the partner's | null 0.245 [0.184, 0.327], p < 10⁻⁴ | sensitivity |
| **N1b** same-surah, outside neighbourhood | endings of the verse's own surah with |Δverse| > 3 | the surah's own rhyme repertoire | whether the ending fits the *local* k = 3 window beyond the surah-level pattern | null 0.774 [0.673, 0.878], p = 0.42 | **diagnostic** — see interpretation |
| **N2** phonologically matched | all verse endings sharing (final-consonant sonorant class, pre-final vowel length, final-word letter-length bucket) | coarse phonological shape of the ending | the exact final phoneme | null 0.530 [0.408, 0.633], p < 10⁻⁴ | sensitivity |

**Pair-level versions.** Replacing the partner's ending by a random alternative from the pool and recomputing the pair outcome: N0 → 0.937 of decisive pairs favour the original (95% 0.842–1.0); N3 → 0.866 (0.765–1.0). These compare the original ending with *random* alternatives; the primary analysis compares it with the partner's *actual* ending (0.88 of decisive pairs).

**Interpretation of N1b.** When alternatives are drawn from the same surah outside the window, the null reproduces the observed rate. This is expected and informative: Quranic surahs tend to maintain a dominant rhyme, so an ending that fits the surah's repertoire fits the local window too. The phenomenon is therefore **surah-level rhyme accommodation**, not a fine-grained local effect beyond the surah pattern. N1b is not a test of the main hypothesis (which compares the original ending to the partner's or to frequency-matched alternatives) and is reported to make the scope of the claim precise.

**Scope statement.** All nulls are conditional on the 30 eligible pairs produced by the frozen extraction rule (3-word seed, similarity 0.70–0.99, ≥ 5 words, cross-surah, distinguishable final phoneme). Generalization is to near-duplicate cross-surah verse pairs in the Hafs text under these rules; robustness across extraction settings is reported in `FASILA_ROBUSTNESS.md`.
