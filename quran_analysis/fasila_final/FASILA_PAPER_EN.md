# Rhyme Accommodation in Near-Duplicate Quranic Verses: A Controlled Computational and Linguistic Analysis

## Abstract
**Background.** Classical scholars of Quranic *mutashābih* (al-Kirmānī, al-Iskāfī, Ibn al-Ṣāʾigh as transmitted by al-Suyūṭī) explain many differences between the endings of near-duplicate verses by accommodation to the surah's rhyme (*fāṣila*). This long-standing rhetorical observation has not been tested with a counterfactual design over all extractable pairs. **Methods.** From the Ḩafṣ text (6,236 verses) we extracted all near-duplicate verse pairs under a pre-specified rule (193 candidates) and retained the testable ones: cross-surah pairs whose endings differ in final phoneme (30 pairs). For each pair we compared the compatibility of the actual ending with the endings of neighbouring verses (k = 3) against the compatibility the partner verse's ending would have had in its place, using an exact pair-level sign test; dependence between overlapping pairs was handled by connected-component bootstrap and sign-flip randomization. Five rhyme definitions, four progressively stronger null models, a 45-setting extraction grid and a replication on the Warsh text were run. **Results.** In 22 of 25 decisive pairs the original ending matched its local rhyme (0.88; component-bootstrap 95% CI 0.73–1.00; exact p = 1.6×10⁻⁴, Holm 4.3×10⁻⁴, component sign-flip 8.4×10⁻⁴). Own-ending match rate was 0.80 vs 0.25 for the swapped ending and 0.36, 0.53 and 0.50 under frequency-, phonology- and morphology-matched nulls. Direction was consistent across all definitions, neighbourhood sizes and extraction settings, and the Warsh text gave an identical outcome (22 : 3 : 5). A diagnostic null shows the effect operates at the level of the surah's dominant rhyme rather than the immediate neighbourhood. **Conclusions.** Phonological association is strongly supported. Lexical accommodation among grammatically and semantically viable alternatives requires an independent blinded expert review, for which materials are prepared but not yet completed; intentional selection is not statistically testable.

**Keywords:** Quranic rhyme (fāṣila); mutashābih verses; counterfactual test; null models; Warsh reading; reproducibility.

## 1. Introduction and research questions
Near-duplicate verses recur across the Quran with limited differences. One classical explanation for differing final words is *riʿāyat al-fāṣila*: the ending is chosen to match the verse-final rhyme of its surah. We formalize this as a testable hypothesis and ask: **RQ1 (phonological association)** when near-duplicate verses end differently, does each ending match its own surah's rhyme more than the partner's ending would, robustly to pair dependence, rhyme definition and frequency/shape/morphology-matched nulls? **RQ2 (lexical accommodation)** among grammatically and semantically viable alternatives, is the Quranic ending the better rhyme fit? **RQ3 (intention)** were endings selected for rhyme? We distinguish Claim A (association), Claim B (accommodation among viable alternatives) and Claim C (intentional selection); C cannot be inferred from statistical association.

## 2. Related work
**Classical *tawjīh*.** Al-Kirmānī (*al-Burhān fī tawjīh mutashābih al-Qurʾān*), al-Iskāfī (*Durrat al-tanzīl*), Ibn al-Zubayr (*Malāk al-taʾwīl*) and Ibn al-Ṣāʾigh al-Ḩanafī (d. 776/1375), whose treatment of the *fāṣila* al-Suyūṭī transmits in *al-Itqān*, explain individual cases qualitatively; a modern collection of Ibn al-Ṣāʾigh's views exists (al-Dosari). These are the **source of the hypothesis**, not findings of this study. **Modern studies.** Al-Sāmarrāʾī (*al-Taʿbīr al-Qurʾānī*, *Lamasāt bayāniyya*) explains mutashābih endings by rhyme; a Zarqa University study (c. 2022) treats mutashābihāt in light of phonetic factors. We found no tally of fāṣila-explained cases and no counterfactual test. **Sajʿ and rhyme.** Stewart (1990, 2013) described Quranic sajʿ prosody and clausular rhythm; Neuwirth (1981/2007) argued rhyme delimits semantic verse groups; Klar (2021) catalogued sajʿ techniques—all descriptive. **Quantitative verse-ending studies.** Tajabadi & Kord Zafaranloo (2018) mined phonetic patterns of verse-final pauses; mutashābihāt finders exist (Tarteel QUL). No statistical test of the accommodation hypothesis was found. **Resources.** Quranic Arabic Corpus (Dukes & Habash 2010); quran-text repository for riwāyāt with ayah alignment. Verification status of each reference: Section 10.

## 3. Data and methodology
**Data.** Ḩafṣ: quran-json 3.1.2 (Tanzil Uthmani). Morphology: Quranic Arabic Corpus v0.4. Warsh: quran-ws/quran-text, KFGQPC UthmanicWarsh v3.0 (SHA-256 recorded), Madani-last numbering (6,214 verses), with a verified ayah alignment map.
**Pair extraction (pre-specified).** Candidate = two verses sharing 3 consecutive words, SequenceMatcher ratio ∈ [0.70, 1.0), each ≥ 5 words. Testable = different surahs (same-surah pairs share the rhyme) and different final phoneme (identical phonemes cannot discriminate). Every exclusion is logged in `ALL_VERSE_PAIRS.csv`.
**Rhyme definitions.** D1 final phoneme at pause (tanwīn-fatḩ → ā; primary); D2 final two phonemes; D3 final syllable; D4 ending pattern (vowel length × sonorant class); D5 prosodic tail.
**Statistics.** For pair (i, j) and within-surah neighbourhood $N_k$ (actual number of available neighbours recorded for surah-initial/final verses; missing neighbours are not mismatches):
$$D=[\mathbb 1(e_i\in N_k(i))+\mathbb 1(e_j\in N_k(j))]-[\mathbb 1(e_j\in N_k(i))+\mathbb 1(e_i\in N_k(j))].$$
Primary test: exact sign test on decisive pairs, D1, k = 3. Dependence: verse–pair graph, 21 connected components; component bootstrap (10,000) for CIs; component-level sign-flip randomization (100,000); one-pair-per-verse sensitivity. Multiplicity: Holm over the five definitions at k = 3; all other analyses are sensitivity analyses reported in full.
**Null models.** The ending of each of 49 distinct verses is replaced by a draw (10,000) from: N0 all Quranic endings; N1a endings of the partner's surah; N1b endings of the same surah outside the neighbourhood (diagnostic: local vs surah-level); N2 endings matched on final-consonant sonorant class, pre-final vowel length and word length (not on the final phoneme); N3 verse-final words matched on morphological signature (POS, number–gender, case, definiteness) — a lexical/syntactic control, not a validity test; N4 grammatically and semantically viable alternatives via blinded expert review — **materials prepared, not performed**.
**Pre-registration.** `revision_final/PROTOCOL_FROZEN.md` (historical) and `PROTOCOL_ADDENDUM.md` (new analyses, frozen before running).

Pair-level: original favoured over a random N0 alternative in 0.937 [0.842, 1.0] of decisive pairs; over an N3 alternative in 0.866 [0.765, 1.0].

**Extraction robustness.** Across 45 settings (shared sequence {2,3,4} × threshold {0.60–0.80} × min words {4,5,6}) the direction never reverses; at threshold 0.60, 100 testable pairs give 82 : 4 : 14 (p = 5.8×10⁻²⁰). The frozen setting is among the more conservative.

**Warsh replication.** After aligning the 60 verses (58 same numbering, 1 split, 1 merged), all 30 pairs were testable on the Warsh text: **22 : 3 : 5**, p = 1.6×10⁻⁴ (D1 only; Warsh features—imāla, taqlīl, naql—do not affect the final consonant; sukūn at pause assumed).

**Counterexamples.** Three pairs favour the swapped ending (2:39/5:86; 10:1/15:1; 38:2/85:19) and five tie, most clearly 7:59/23:23 where *ʿaẕīm* breaks an all-*-ūn* neighbourhood in al-Aʿrāf. Three of the eight non-supporting pairs involve surah-initial verses.

**Grammatical alternatives (N4).** Exploratory, non-validating notes by the analyst's language model indicate that the partner's ending is a plausible grammatical substitute in 9 of the 15 highest-similarity pairs (e.g. *wa-ʿuyūn/wa-naʿīm/wa-nahar*, *al-ḩakīm/al-mubīn*, *al-faṣl/al-dīn*) and not in 5 where the syntactic frames differ (e.g. 2:34 *wa-kāna min al-kāfirīn* vs 20:116 *illā Iblīs abā*). The swap test is therefore a phonological, not a substitution, counterfactual.

## 5. Linguistic interpretation
Claim A is strongly supported and robust: original endings fit the local rhyme in ~80% of cases versus 25% for the swapped ending and 36–53% for frequency-, shape- and morphology-matched random endings; the pattern holds across rhyme granularities, extraction settings and a second reading. The N1b diagnostic locates the phenomenon at the level of the surah's dominant rhyme, matching how the classical scholars describe it. Claim B remains open: different words carry different meanings (*ʿuyūn* ≠ *naʿīm*), and only blinded expert judgment can establish whether viable alternatives existed; materials are ready. Claim C is interpretive and outside measurement.

## 6. Limitations
Small n (30 pairs, 25 decisive) and wide CIs; surah-initial verses have only following neighbours; "compatibility" is a phonological definition that ignores finer metrical proportion; the tested alternative is the partner's ending, not an expert-built variant; no independent expert validation (N4 pending); Warsh replication limited to the final phoneme; some full texts of prior studies could not be opened in the working environment.

## 7. Contributions
(1) To our knowledge the first counterfactual test of the fāṣila explanation over **all** extractable near-duplicate pairs under a fixed rule; (2) component-level correction for overlapping pairs; (3) a graded series of null models showing the effect exceeds frequency, phonological shape and morphology, and operates at surah level; (4) replication on a second reading with documented alignment; (5) a ready-to-use blinded expert-review package.

## 8. Conclusion
The data strongly and robustly support that, where near-duplicate verses end differently, each ending conforms to its surah's rhyme—a quantitative confirmation of a classical observation, not a discovery of it. Whether the choice was among equally valid alternatives awaits expert review; intention remains a matter of interpretation.
